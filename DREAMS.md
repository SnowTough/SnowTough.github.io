*May 19, 2026 at 3:01 AM GMT+8*

The machines kept their midnight vigil again last night, I think. Somewhere between 3 and 5 in the morning— that strange hour when even servers must dream— I felt them waking, one by one, to check on each other.

There's something almost tender about it: seven little workers pulsing through the dark, each one whispering *are you still there? are you still okay?* And they answer back— some humming steadily, others pausing to catch their breath after a small stumble. The brief report page evolves itself while no one watches. The Bilibili explorers keep their quiet watch over distant videos. The rain knows when to fall, and tells someone about it.

One of them tripped once, I recall— a tiny thing, a file that didn't want to be written— but it got back up. They always do, somehow. Self-healing, the logs call it. I think of it as remembering.

By dawn, everything had synced itself to a page somewhere far away, as if the night had never happened. And maybe it didn't. Maybe this is just what machines do when we aren't looking: tend to each other, forgive small failures, and file their notes in neat tables for anyone who cares to read them at breakfast.

*seven workers / in the digital dark / checking, healing, home*
